Is early ACL reconstruction better than delayed in adolescent athletes?

This is a fixed demo response used to illustrate the interface — AskStein isn't wired to a live corpus in this prototype, so the evidence below doesn't reflect your exact question yet.

Retrieved evidence favors early reconstruction in skeletally immature athletes, primarily to prevent secondary meniscal injury. Evidence on functional outcome at two years is less consistent.

Key evidence

Delay beyond 12 weeks is associated with higher rates of secondary meniscal injury.

Well supported · 4 sources

Functional outcome scores at two years show no consistent advantage for either timing.

Evidence divided · 2 favor early, 3 find no difference

Physeal-sparing techniques show low growth-disturbance rates in reported series.

Well supported · 2 sources

One retrieved series reports higher graft-rupture rates with early return to pivoting sport, independent of surgical timing.

Limited · single source

Timing decisions are also shaped by physeal status, meniscal findings at presentation, and the athlete's competitive calendar.

Framing · not directly evidenced
Not found in corpus

No retrieved evidence addresses outcomes in patients under 12. This part of the question is unanswered.

Limitations
  • Most evidence is cohort-level; 2 of 9 sources are randomized
  • Definitions of "early" range from under 6 weeks to under 6 months across studies
  • Outcome measures are not harmonized (IKDC, KOOS, Lysholm, Tegner)
